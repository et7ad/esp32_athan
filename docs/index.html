A prayer clock that follows your mosque's timetable

It calls the athan at your mosque's times, shows the next prayer from across the room and plays Quran radio. A whole year of times lives in its own memory, so the athan keeps coming without internet.

How the prayer times reach a clock

Every mosque publishes its times its own way. Rather than teach the clock all of them, the work is split in two: getting each mosque's times right happens here, once, and the clock only has to read one simple file.

1

Your mosque publishes its timetable

A PDF, a web page, a spreadsheet: whatever it already uses.

2

It becomes one checked file here

Scripts turn the timetable into one file per year, in one format, and check every day of it before it is published on GitHub.

3

Every clock downloads its year

Once a year, kept in its own memory. It never contacts the mosque's website, so a site that changes or goes offline can't affect it.

What it does

Plug it in, connect it once, and it runs by itself. Everything below can also be done from a phone, on the clock's own web page.

See the screen and the menu

  • mosqueThe athan at every prayerA separate Fajr athan, each at its own volume, and each prayer's athan can be switched off on its own.
  • eventA year of times, offlineOnce it is set, the athan needs no internet. It fetches next year's times by itself in December.
  • radioQuran radioTen stations from this project's list, or any stream link of your own.
  • headphonesYour choice of reciterChoose each sound from the project's library, or upload your own MP3.
  • alarmPre-Fajr Tawashih25 minutes before Fajr it plays a tawashih and can switch a room light on.
  • timerAn hourly tickDuring the hours you choose, from Fajr to Isha for example.
  • smartphoneSet up from a phoneOver Bluetooth, or through the clock's own Wi-Fi hotspot. Works with Home Assistant too.
  • system_updateUpdates when you chooseIt shows a small mark when new firmware is out, and never installs by itself.

A case shaped like a mihrab

The speaker plays through a grille of eight-pointed stars in the arch, and "حي على الصلاة" (come to prayer) is engraved under the screen. It prints in three parts, without supports, in the colours you like.
